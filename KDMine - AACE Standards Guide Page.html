Introduction

What is AACE International?

AACE International (Association for the Advancement of Cost Engineering) is the leading professional organization for cost engineers, estimators, schedulers, and project managers worldwide. Founded in 1956, AACE sets the global standard for cost estimation practice through its Recommended Practice (RPs).

For mining projects, the key reference is AACE RP 47R-11: 'Cost Estimate Classification System — As Applied in Engineering, Procurement, and Construction for the Mining and Mineral Processing Industries.' This document defines five classes of cost estimates, each with specific accuracy ranges, methodologies, and project completion requirements.

KDMine Tip

Choosing the wrong estimate class is one of the most common and costly mistakes in mining project development. A Class 5 estimate used for investment decisions can result in cost overruns of 50–100%. Always match your estimate class to your project stage and decision requirements.

Estimation Process

How KDMine Builds an AACE-Compliant Estimate

Our structured estimating process ensures every deliverable meets AACE standards with full documentation, peer review, and risk-adjusted contingency.

1

Scope Definition & Basis of Estimate (BoE)

We establish the project scope, define the estimate class, document all assumptions, exclusions, and data sources. The BoE is the foundation of every AACE-compliant estimate.

2

Quantity Take-Off (QTO) & Equipment List

Detailed quantity take-off from engineering drawings and specifications. Equipment list compiled from vendor datasheets and our 10,000+ item proprietary database with current market pricing.

3

Direct & Indirect Cost Estimation

ISBL and OSBL direct costs estimated using current labor rates, material costs, and equipment prices. Indirect costs (engineering, procurement, construction management, commissioning) applied per AACE guidelines.

4

Contingency & Risk Analysis

Monte Carlo simulation used to quantify estimate uncertainty. Contingency calculated at the 50th or 80th percentile confidence level per project requirements. Risk register developed with mitigation strategies.

5

Independent Peer Review & Validation

Every estimate is independently reviewed by a senior AACE-certified cost professional not involved in the original preparation. Benchmarked against comparable projects in our database.

6

Final Report & Deliverables

Comprehensive estimate report including: summary sheets, detailed cost breakdown, basis of estimate, assumptions log, risk register, sensitivity analysis, and benchmarking data. Formatted for lender, board, or regulatory submission.

Mining Applications

AACE Standards in Open-Pit Mining Projects

Applying AACE standards to mining projects requires deep understanding of mine-specific cost drivers including stripping ratio, ore grade variability, equipment fleet selection, processing route, and site location factors.

Key Mining Cost Areas Covered by AACE RP 47R-11

Mine development (pre-strip, access roads, dewatering) · Processing plant (crushing, grinding, leaching, flotation) · Infrastructure (power, water, camp, tailings) · Mobile equipment fleet (drills, shovels, trucks, dozers) · Environmental & closure costs · Owner's costs & EPCM fees.

Common Mistakes in Mining Cost Estimation

Underestimating owner's costs (typically 5–10% of CAPEX) · Ignoring location factor adjustments for remote sites · Using outdated equipment pricing databases · Insufficient contingency for greenfield projects · Omitting pre-production operating costs from CAPEX.
